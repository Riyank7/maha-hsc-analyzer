Physics and Chemistry spread marks across many chapters. A short list of two or three chapters does not cover most of those papers. Mathematics gives substantial recurring space to calculus and vectors / geometry. Computer Science I concentrates on C++ and Operating Systems; Computer Science II concentrates on 8085 programming plus its architecture and networking.

English needs a different reading: the recent required paper allocates 34/80 to reading, language study, summary and mind mapping, 14/80 to poetry, 16/80 to writing, and 16/80 to the novel. Reading plus writing accounts for 50/80 = 62.5%. Offered writing alternatives total 48 marks, so an offered-mark ranking is not a prediction of a student’s attainable writing marks. The 2023 poetry printing error accounts for six conditional credit marks, kept in a separate bucket. Contemporaneous report of board clarification.

How to interpret and reproduce the numbers

Modern Physics / Chemistry: 70 attainable versus 98 offered marks; Mathematics: 80 versus 112; CS I / II: 50 versus 94; English: 80 versus 112. Count each offered question and internal alternative once. When separately printed subpart marks exist, retain them. Otherwise keep a joint chapter block and show lower / upper allocation bounds. A group earns a joint block’s marks only if it contains every chapter in that block. The selected majority group is greedy, not a proven minimum set.

English historical section totals use required marks and are never combined with offered chapter marks. Seen prose and poetry activity allocations include language / response tasks embedded in that extract; these are chapter-associated blocks, not pure factual-recall marks. OCR assists reading, while chapter labels and totals are reviewed in scripts/reviewed.py. Subjective crosswalk assignments may need educator review before high-stakes curriculum decisions.
