Methodology

The research window covers 2007–2026 main February/March examinations, including main sittings extending into April, in English. Computer Science I and II are separate papers. Supplementary, repeater, model and prelim papers are excluded.

Marks and optional questions

Chapter shares use all printed / offered marks, including alternatives. Their total can exceed the marks a student can earn. For a 70-mark exam offering 98 marks of questions, shares use 98 as the denominator. Each year is normalized to 100% before averaging, with historical Paper I and II combined within the year. A chapter group exceeds 50% only when all chapters in a joint block are included. Unprinted subpart splits are shown as allocation ranges; upper bounds overlap and cannot be added. The majority group uses a greedy search and is not guaranteed to be the smallest group.

Syllabus changes

Older papers sometimes split Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics into Paper I and II. Related topics are mapped to current chapter names where appropriate; legacy topics stay labeled. Use the 2022–2026 view to inspect the recent syllabus. English requires skills and sections as well as named prose, poems and novels.

Verification

Located means a source lists a paper. Downloaded means a valid local PDF is available. Historical retyped papers without printed dates retain the source archive’s year/session attribution, explicitly noted on their cards. A question review does not independently authenticate that attribution. Question reviews map questions or joint blocks and reconcile their marks. Thirteen older English papers (2008–2020) are reviewed only at section level; their 80 required marks are shown separately from offered chapter rankings. Named historical English literature chapters are not mapped. OCR alone is not verification. Duplicate copies are excluded; distinct paper parts and sets remain separate.

2021 is a cancelled main written examination, not a zero-mark observation. Missing papers and incomplete mappings are excluded. Every result describes the analyzed sample and is not a guarantee of future questions.

The historical Mathematics 2012 sample has Paper I only. It remains available with this limitation. For current preparation, compare the recent view; historical patterns are not future weightage guarantees.
